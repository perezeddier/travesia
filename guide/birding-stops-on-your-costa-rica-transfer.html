Birding Stops You Can Add to Your Transfer

Three places that sit right on Costa Rica's main lodge-to-lodge roads. None of them require a detour worth arguing about, and all three are productive enough to justify the stop on their own.

Why roadside stops work so well here

Costa Rica has a particular birding culture: small family restaurants and cafés in the highlands hang fruit and sugar-water feeders, and the birds turn up. The result is a handful of places where you can stand still with a coffee and see species that would take a serious morning's walk elsewhere.

For someone on a travel day, that is exactly the right shape of birding. You are not committing to a trail or a schedule. You're stopping for an hour or two on a road you were driving anyway.

Below are the three we build into transfers most often. What follows is the planning detail — route, timing, what the place actually is. For the species lists and what a stop costs, see our birding transport page.

1. Soda Cinchona — the Vara Blanca route

WhereOn the Vara Blanca / Cinchona road, north of San José toward Sarapiquí
Fits onSan José → La Fortuna, San José → Sarapiquí, airport → northern lodges
Opens6 a.m.
EntranceNone — it's a restaurant; order breakfast
Allow1–2 hours

A working roadside soda — a small family restaurant — with a feeder balcony overlooking a forested gorge and a waterfall. You sit, you eat, and hummingbirds, barbets and toucanets work the feeders a few metres away. It is one of the easiest places in the country to photograph highland species without a long lens or a long walk.

The catch is geography: the Vara Blanca road is not the fastest way north. Building Cinchona into a transfer means choosing the scenic route deliberately and leaving earlier. Done at 6 or 7 a.m., it turns the first half of a travel day into the best birding of it.

How we handle it: Cinchona is offered as a route option on San José transfers heading north, at the VIP rate, with up to two hours at the stop.

All three photographed at this stop while driving guests — our own pictures, not stock.

2. Bogarín Trail — inside La Fortuna

WhereLa Fortuna town, walking distance from the centre
Fits onAny arrival into or departure from La Fortuna
EntranceYes — private reserve, paid on site
Terrain~2.5 km of flat, wheelchair-accessible trail
Allow1–2 hours, longer for the full loop

A regenerated private reserve on the edge of town — former pasture that was allowed to come back, and did so spectacularly. It now holds over 400 species on eBird and is widely cited as the most reliable place in Costa Rica for Uniform Crake. It is also one of the country's best-known spots for sloths, with dozens resident on the property.

What makes it unusual for a travel day: it's in town. There is no detour at all. You can bird it on the morning you leave La Fortuna, or on the afternoon you arrive, without touching the drive time.

The flat, accessible trail also makes it the right choice for mixed-mobility groups — something that is genuinely hard to find at Costa Rican birding sites.

How we handle it: because it's on the route, we include the Bogarín stop as a courtesy on La Fortuna transfers. The reserve's own entrance fee is paid there.

3. Café Colibrí — Monteverde

WhereMonteverde, by the reserve entrance area
Fits onAny Monteverde arrival or departure
EntranceNone — it's a café
Allow45 minutes to an hour

The hummingbird stop. A café with a bank of feeders that draws twenty or more hummingbird species — Violet Sabrewing, Green-crowned Brilliant, Green Hermit, Lesser Violetear, Coppery-headed Emerald among them — at close enough range that people photograph them on phones.

It is not a substitute for the Monteverde reserve, and it isn't meant to be. It's a high-density, low-effort stop that fits into the gap between checking out and leaving, or the gap between arriving and your first trail.

How we handle it: included as a courtesy stop on Monteverde transfers.

One more, on the southern side

Not a birding site, and we won't pretend otherwise: Mi Rancho is a roadside café and restaurant with wide mountain views, and it's our usual lunch stop on the La Fortuna → San José and La Fortuna → Manuel Antonio drives. What makes it worth a line here is that a toucan comes to the tables often enough that the staff treat him as a resident.

It's the kind of thing you can't plan for and can't guarantee. But if your route goes that way, it costs you nothing to stop for lunch and see.

The lodge walk comes first. Most birding lodges sell their guided morning walk separately from the room. Book it, find out what time it finishes, and set your departure from that. A roadside stop is a bonus on the way — it shouldn't cost you the lodge's own grounds at dawn.

What these stops are not

Honest framing, because it matters: these are feeder stations and an easy trail, not a guided birding tour. Nobody is going to call out a call note for you. What they offer is access — being in the right place, at the right hour, with time to stand still.

If you want species identification, hire a birding guide. Costa Rica has excellent ones, and we can point you toward guides we work with. What we do is get you there at the hour that works, with your gear in the cabin and no clock running.
