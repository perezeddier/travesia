The Best Time to Visit Costa Rica

There isn't really a "bad" time — just trade-offs. Here's how the seasons work, region by region, so you can pick the dates that fit the trip you want.

Costa Rica has two seasons, and they're about rain rather than temperature — near the coast it stays warm (roughly 26–32 °C / 79–90 °F) all year. What changes is how much it rains, how green the landscape is, and how busy the country feels.

Dry season (December–April): sunshine and peak season

These are the driest, sunniest months and the most popular time to travel. Expect blue skies, easy beach days and reliable road conditions — but also the highest prices and the busiest towns, especially around Christmas, New Year and Easter (Semana Santa). If you want guaranteed sun and don't mind booking early, this is your window.

Green season (May–November): fewer crowds, lower prices

Locals call the rainy months the "green season," and for good reason — the forests are lush, the waterfalls are full, and wildlife is active. Mornings are usually bright, with rain arriving in the afternoon or evening. You'll find noticeably lower prices, more space at the top sights, and a country at its greenest. September and October are the wettest overall (and the quietest), though the Caribbean side often stays sunnier then.

Good to know: the Caribbean coast (Puerto Viejo, Tortuguero) runs on its own rhythm — its driest, sunniest stretches are often September–October, exactly when the Pacific is at its wettest.

When should you go?

  • Sun-first beach trip: December to April.
  • Best value & fewest crowds: May, June or November.
  • Green landscapes & waterfalls at their fullest: the green season, any month.
  • Caribbean coast: aim for September–October.

How the season affects getting around

In the green season, afternoon rain can make mountain roads (like the routes to Monteverde or over the mountains to the Pacific) slower and slicker, and rivers can rise.
