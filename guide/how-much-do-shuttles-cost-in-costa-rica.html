How Much Do Shuttles Cost in Costa Rica? Real 2026 Prices

No "starting from" teasers — these are the actual prices we charge, per vehicle, taxes included. Plus how shared shuttles compare and the hidden fees to watch for.

The one thing to understand: per vehicle vs per person

Costa Rica has two kinds of shuttles, and they're priced differently:

  • Private shuttles are priced per vehicle — the price is the same whether 1 or 5 people ride. Door-to-door, your own schedule, your own van.
  • Shared shuttles are priced per person (typically $54–75 each) — fixed timetables, multiple hotel stops, you ride with other travelers.

That's why the math flips with group size: solo travelers often save with shared; from about 3 people, private usually costs the same or less — and it's a different level of comfort.

Do the math for your group

A real example — SJO → La Fortuna, family of four:

  • Shared shuttle: 4 × $54–75 = $216–300, fixed departure time, several hotel stops on the way.
  • Private shuttle: $220 total, leaves when your flight lands, stops when your kids need a restroom, drops you at your hotel door.

Same money — completely different day. For couples, shared is cheaper on paper ($108–150 vs $220); the private premium buys flexibility, zero waiting and courtesy stops.

What affects the price?

  • Distance and terrain — mountain routes take longer per kilometer.
  • Vehicle size — bigger vans cost more per vehicle, less per seat.
  • What does NOT change it: number of passengers (up to the van's capacity), luggage, child seats, or paying by card — at least with us.

Hidden fees to watch for (anywhere you book)

  • "+13% VAT at checkout" — some companies quote pre-tax prices that grow at payment. Ask if the price is final.
  • Per-person pricing dressed up as private — check whether a "private" quote multiplies by passengers.
  • Luggage or surfboard fees, night surcharges — ask before you book.
  • Card details over chat or email — never. A serious company sends you to a secure payment page.
