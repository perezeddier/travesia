The problem every birder in Costa Rica knows

You've built a trip around habitats: cloud forest, Caribbean slope, dry forest. But every time you change lodges, the same thing happens — breakfast, load the van, four hours on the road, arrive after lunch. The best hours of the day went to a highway.

It doesn't have to work that way. Costa Rica's roads run right past some of the most productive feeder stations in the country. A transfer that's planned around them turns a travel day into a birding day.

How it works

  1. Tell us where you're staying and where you're going next. We know the lodges on these routes — including which ones run their own guided morning walk, so we can time the pickup around it instead of cutting your morning short.
  2. We plan the drive around a birding stop. Leaving La Fortuna for San José, for example, we can take the Vara Blanca route and stop at Soda Cinchona — feeders, a balcony over the waterfall, and real time to watch.
  3. You bird. We wait. Private vehicle, no other passengers, no schedule to keep. When you're ready, we carry on.

The three stops we run

These are the ones we actually drive to, not a list copied off a map.

Soda Cinchona (Colibrí Café) — on the Vara Blanca route

A roadside soda on the Caribbean slope, about ten minutes downhill from La Paz Waterfall Gardens, with hummingbird and fruit feeders on a balcony overlooking the San Fernando waterfall. For its size it is one of the most productive feeder stations in the country.

Regulars at the feeders: Green Thorntail · White-bellied Mountain-gem · Coppery-headed Emerald (Costa Rica endemic) · Red-headed Barbet · Northern Emerald-Toucanet · Prong-billed Barbet · Buff-fronted Quail-Dove. The café opens at 6 a.m. and mornings are best.

Bogarín Trail — in La Fortuna

A 16-hectare second-growth reserve a few minutes from the centre of La Fortuna, with 2.5 km of wheelchair-accessible trail and more than 400 species recorded on eBird. It's self-guided, and a good share of the birds show up around the feeders and open areas without much walking. It's also the best sloth spot in the area, with thirty-plus resident animals — useful if you're travelling with non-birders.

Known for: Uniform Crake (this is the most reliable site in Costa Rica for it) · Rufous-tailed Jacamar · Black-crested Coquette · Rufous-winged Woodpecker · Barred Antshrike · Rufous Motmot · Orange-billed Sparrow · honeycreepers, plus wintering Golden-winged, Hooded, Chestnut-sided and Mourning Warblers.

Café Colibrí — at the Monteverde reserve entrance

The hummingbird gallery right at the entrance to the Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve: gardens, a bank of feeders, and a café. More than twenty hummingbird species come through, often at arm's length — it's the easiest photography of the trip for most guests.

Regulars: Violet Sabrewing · Green-crowned Brilliant · Green Hermit · Lesser Violetear · Coppery-headed Emerald (Costa Rica endemic).

What we are — and what we're not

We're a driver, not a birding guide. We don't identify species for you and we don't carry a scope. What we do is know the roads, the stops, the timing, and we're comfortable waiting while you work a flock or wait out a quail-dove.

Plenty of our birding guests travel with their own guide, or bird independently. If you'd like a guide, we can connect you with local birding guides we work with — tell us the region and the kind of birding you're after.

A practical tip about lodges

Most birding lodges sell their guided morning walk separately from the room — it's not automatically included. Book it directly with the lodge, then send us your finish time. We'll set the pickup around it so you never cut a morning short to catch a ride.

Frequently asked questions

Do you provide a birding guide?
No. We are a private transport company, not a birding guide service. We do not identify species for you or carry a scope. What we do is know the routes, the stops and the timing, and wait as long as you need. If you are travelling without a guide, we can connect you with local birding guides we work with.
How much does a birding stop cost?
Stops that sit directly on your route, such as Bogarín Trail in La Fortuna or Café Colibrí in Monteverde, are included at no extra cost — just tell us when you book. The Vara Blanca route with a stop at Soda Cinchona is a genuine detour and longer drive, so it is $80 on top of the transfer price and includes up to 2 hours at the stop.
Can you pick us up early for dawn birding?
Yes. Our transfers are private, so the departure time is yours. Most birding transfers leave around 8 a.m. because guests bird their lodge grounds first, but we regularly run earlier departures when the plan calls for it.
Is the entrance fee to Bogarín Trail included?
No. Bogarín Trail charges its own entrance fee, paid directly at the trail. We bring you there, park, and wait while you walk it.
Do the lodges include morning birding?
Most birding lodges sell their guided morning walk separately from the room. Book it directly with the lodge, then tell us your finish time and we plan the pickup around it so you never have to cut a morning short.
How many people fit, with luggage and optical gear?
Three vehicle sizes: up to 5, up to 9 and up to 12 passengers. Scopes, tripods and camera cases travel inside with you rather than buried under suitcases — tell us what you are bringing and we assign the right vehicle.
